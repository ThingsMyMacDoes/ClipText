Getting Started with ClipText

ClipText copies text from anywhere on your Mac's screen: screenshots, images, paused videos, scanned PDFs, and apps that won't let you select text. You select an area, ClipText reads the text in it, and the text goes onto your clipboard, ready to paste.

All text recognition happens on your Mac using Apple's Vision technology. ClipText has no internet access and saves none of your captures.

Requirements: macOS 27 or later.

1. Find ClipText in your menu bar

ClipText has no Dock icon and no window. After you open it, look for its icon in the menu bar at the top right of your screen: a square with a letter T inside and a small + at its bottom-right corner.

The first time you open ClipText, a Welcome window explains this. You can open it again from the right-click menu (Welcome Guide…).

If you can't see the icon, your menu bar may be full. See Troubleshooting.

2. Allow Screen Recording (first time only)

The first time you capture, macOS asks for permission for ClipText to record your screen. You can also start this from the Welcome window. ClipText needs this to see the text on screen. It doesn't record video and doesn't send anything anywhere.

  1. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording.
  2. Turn on ClipText. It may also be listed as screencapture.
  3. If macOS asks, quit and reopen ClipText.

macOS may also ask whether ClipText may read the clipboard. Allow this. ClipText reads the screenshot from the clipboard to recognise the text, and keeps a temporary copy of what you had copied so it can put it back if no text is found.

3. Capture text

  1. Click the ClipText icon in the menu bar, or press ⇧⌘2 (Shift-Command-2) from any app.
  2. Your pointer becomes crosshairs, just like the built-in ⌘⇧4 screenshot tool.
  3. Drag a box around the text you want, then release.
  4. ClipText reads the text and copies it to your clipboard.
  5. Paste it anywhere with ⌘V.

A short message appears near the top of the screen:

MessageMeaning
✅ Text copied to clipboardSuccess. The first lines of the copied text are shown underneath, unless you've turned off Show Text Preview.
⚠️ No text found in selectionClipText couldn't find readable text. Your clipboard is put back the way it was.
⚠️ Text capture failedSomething went wrong. The message includes details. Your clipboard is put back the way it was.

If VoiceOver is on, these messages are also spoken.

Selection tips

  • Press Space while selecting to switch to window mode, then click a window to capture all of it.
  • Press Esc to cancel. Nothing is captured and your clipboard stays as it was.
  • Line breaks are kept, so each line of text on screen becomes its own line.

4. The options menu

Right-click the menu bar icon:

  • Capture Text (⇧⌘2): starts a capture, the same as left-clicking.
  • Show Text Preview: turn this off to hide the copied text from the confirmation message, for example while sharing your screen. ClipText remembers your choice.
  • Welcome Guide…: shows the welcome window again.
  • Help & FAQ…: opens help and answers to common questions. Works offline.
  • About ClipText: shows the version number and copyright.
  • Privacy Policy and Support: open these pages on the ClipText website in your browser.
  • Quit: closes ClipText. To use it again, open it from your Applications folder.

5. Get the best results

  • Select tightly. Include only the text you want.
  • Bigger is better. Zoom in first (for example ⌘+ in a browser) for small text.
  • Contrast helps. Dark text on a light background, or the reverse, works best.
  • Language is detected automatically. Proofread text in less common languages and scripts carefully.
  • Proofread. Always check important things like phone numbers, codes and passwords.

Good to know

  • A successful capture replaces what was on your clipboard.
  • Captures are silent. There's no shutter sound.
  • Only one capture runs at a time.
  • ClipText's menus, messages, Welcome window and Help are in English or French, following your Mac's language setting.
  • The preview message is visible on screen for about three seconds. Keep this in mind when screen sharing, or turn off Show Text Preview.

Troubleshooting ClipText

ProblemTry this
I can't see the menu bar iconYour menu bar might be full, or the icon may be hidden behind the camera housing on Macs with a notch. Quit some other menu bar apps, or hold ⌘ and drag icons to rearrange them. Check that ClipText is running: open it again from Applications. You can still capture with ⇧⌘2.
"No text found in selection" every timeScreen Recording permission is probably off. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording and turn on ClipText (or "screencapture"). Then quit and reopen ClipText.
"No text found" for one particular areaThe text may be too small, blurry or low-contrast. Zoom in and try again. Handwriting and stylised fonts may not be recognised.
My clipboard changed even though no text was foundClipText puts back what you had copied before. Content that the original app only provides on demand may come back in fewer formats (for example plain text instead of formatted text). If that happens, copy it again from the original app.
Recognised text has mistakesSelect a smaller, sharper area, or zoom in before capturing. Check characters that look alike, such as 0/O and 1/l/I.
Text in another language is inaccurateClipText detects the language automatically. For mixed-language selections, try capturing one language at a time.
Capturing a video or streaming service finds no textSome apps and websites protect their video content, and macOS screenshots of it come out blank. ClipText can only read what macOS's screenshot tool can capture.
Lines are in the wrong orderMulti-column layouts and tables can confuse the reading order. Capture one column at a time.
Nothing happens when I clickA capture may still be running. Press Esc, then try again.
"Text capture failed"Note the details shown in the message and try again. If it keeps happening, contact support with the message text and your macOS version.
macOS asks about clipboard accessClipText reads the screenshot from the clipboard to recognise its text, and keeps a temporary copy of what you had copied so it can put it back. Allow access, or captures won't work.
⇧⌘2 doesn't start a captureCheck that ClipText is running. Another app may be using the same shortcut. Quit that app, or click the menu bar icon instead.
I don't want the copied text to appear on screenRight-click the menu bar icon and turn off Show Text Preview.
I want to remove ClipText completelyRight-click the icon → Quit, then drag ClipText from Applications to the Bin. ClipText stores no captures. It only keeps two settings (the text preview choice and whether you've seen the welcome window). To remove those too, delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/[ACTION REQUIRED: Bundle identifier] (in Finder: Go → Go to Folder…). Optionally, remove it from the Screen Recording list in System Settings.

Still stuck? Right-click the menu bar icon and choose Help & FAQ…, or see Contact Support.
