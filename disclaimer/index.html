Intended use

ClipText is a convenience tool for copying text that is visible on your Mac's screen. It is designed for everyday productivity tasks. It is not designed or tested for situations where an error in recognised text could cause serious harm, such as medical dosages, legal filings, financial transactions or safety-critical instructions. For such uses, always check the recognised text against the original.

Accuracy of text recognition

ClipText uses Apple's on-device Vision framework. Recognition is automatic and may:

  • misread characters (for example 0/O, 1/l/I, rn/m),
  • miss text that is small, blurred, stylised, low-contrast, rotated or handwritten,
  • produce lower accuracy for some languages and scripts, or for text that mixes languages (ClipText detects the language automatically),
  • change line order for complex layouts such as columns and tables.

Always proofread important text, such as phone numbers, IBANs, codes and passwords, before using it.

Clipboard and data loss

ClipText does not save any captures. A successful capture replaces what was on your clipboard. If no text is found or a capture fails, ClipText puts back the clipboard contents you had before. This is done on a best-effort basis: some content that apps only provide on request, or in special formats, may not be restored exactly. Save anything important before capturing. ClipText offers no backup, because it keeps nothing to back up.

Availability and compatibility

ClipText runs entirely on your Mac and does not depend on any online service. It relies on macOS features (the system screenshot tool, Screen Recording permission, the clipboard and global keyboard shortcuts). Apple may change these in future macOS releases. We will make reasonable efforts to keep ClipText working, but cannot guarantee compatibility with every future macOS version.

Keyboard shortcut

ClipText uses the shortcut ⇧⌘2 while it is running. If another app uses the same shortcut, one of them may not respond to it.
